The 3 Core Steps of Your Project Feature

Think of Method 1 as a three-step assembly line:
1. The AI Step (Depth Estimation): Turns flat video frames into 3D distances.
2. The Math Step (Point Cloud Integration): Groups those distances into a digital 3D model (mesh).
3. The Calculation Step (Volume Subtraction): Measures the empty space left over.

Step 1: The AI Step (Predicting Depth)

A normal video frame is just a flat 2D grid of colored pixels (X and Y coordinates). The AI's job is to predict the Z coordinate (depth)—how far away each pixel is from the phone camera.
• The Model to Use: Depth Anything V2 (or its lightweight versions like depth_anything_v2_vits). It is fully open-source, highly accurate, and has pre-built Python code that is beginner-friendly.
• What it does: You feed it a frame from the video, and it outputs a grayscale image called a Depth Map. In this map, bright white pixels mean "very close to the camera" and dark black pixels mean "far away at the bottom of the container."

Step 2: The Math Step (Building the 3D Point Cloud)

Once you have the X, Y, and Z (depth) coordinates for your video frames, you need to stitch them together into a single 3D object.
• The Library to Use: Open3D (a fantastic Python library for 3D data).
• What it does: It takes the depth maps and projects them into 3D space, creating a Point Cloud (a cloud of millions of tiny dots representing the exact surface of the cargo inside the container). If the user moved their phone around, Open3D can align the frames to create a highly accurate, wrap-around view of the cargo heap.

Step 3: The Calculation Step (Finding the Remaining Volume)

Now you have a digital 3D model of the cargo surface, but you need to know the empty space above it.
• The Setup: In your code, you will define the "Virtual Container." For example, you tell the program: "My container is a perfect cube that is 2 meters wide, 2 meters long, and 3 meters tall."
• The Calculation:
	1. The code calculates the total volume of the empty virtual container (2 × 2 × 3 = 12 m³).
	2. It looks at your AI-generated Point Cloud and calculates the volume of the cargo piles underneath it.
	3. It subtracts the two: Remaining Volume = Total Container Volume - Cargo Volume.
